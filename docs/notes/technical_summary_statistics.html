Technical Appendix: Summary Statistics

Author
Affiliation

Peter Sørensen

Aarhus University

This appendix develops the summary-statistics representation used by sblr from the individual-level linear model. It explains why cross-products are sufficient for BLR-style marker updates, how BED-derived statistics and sparse LD are combined by the CSR samplers, and which assumptions are required for single- and multi-trait analyses.

The central principle is that the Gaussian regression likelihood depends on the observed data through a small set of cross-products. Replacing \(X\) and \(y\) with those cross-products can preserve the marker-update algebra, but only when marker order, sample definition, scaling, and LD are compatible.

Individual-Level Model and Cross-Product Sufficiency

For one centered phenotype, consider

\[ y=Xb+e, \qquad e\sim N(0,v_eI_n), \]

where \(X\) is an \(n\times m\) genotype matrix and \(b\) is an \(m\)-vector of marker effects. Conditional on \(b\) and \(v_e\), the Gaussian log likelihood is, up to constants,

\[ \log p(y\mid b,v_e) = -\frac{1}{2v_e}(y-Xb)^\top(y-Xb). \]

Expanding the residual sum of squares gives

\[ \begin{aligned} (y-Xb)^\top(y-Xb) &= y^\top y -2b^\top X^\top y +b^\top X^\top Xb. \end{aligned} \]

Thus, the likelihood does not require repeated access to every individual observation once the following quantities are available:

\[ y^\top y, \qquad X^\top y, \qquad X^\top X, \qquad n. \]

The prior can still require additional parameters, but BLR marker likelihood calculations can be expressed through these cross-products. This is the sense in which they are sufficient for the Gaussian regression layer.

Single-Trait Summary-Statistic Notation

For one trait, define

\[ wy=X^\top y, \qquad XX=X^\top X, \qquad yy=y^\top y. \]

The diagonal genotype cross-products are

\[ ww_i=XX_{ii}=x_i^\top x_i. \]

In sblr interfaces:

  • wy is the marker-phenotype cross-product vector;
  • ww is the vector containing the diagonal of \(XX\);
  • yy is the phenotype sum of squares;
  • the sparse-LD object represents retained off-diagonal structure; and
  • \(n\) is the sample size associated with these quantities.

The dense matrix \(XX\) can be written as

\[ XX=D+O, \]

where \(D=\operatorname{diag}(ww)\) and \(O\) contains off-diagonal marker cross-products. The CSR samplers keep \(D\) explicitly through ww and reconstruct a sparse approximation to \(O\) from disk-backed LD correlations.

Residual Scores and Marker Updates

Define the marker-scale residual score vector

\[ r = X^\top(y-Xb) = wy-XXb. \]

This is not the individual-level residual vector \(y-Xb\). It is its projection onto all marker columns.

For marker \(i\), adding the current marker effect back to its score gives

\[ s_i=r_i+ww_i b_i. \]

Because

\[ r_i = x_i^\top y -x_i^\top x_i b_i -\sum_{j\ne i}x_i^\top x_jb_j, \]

the quantity \(s_i\) equals

\[ s_i = x_i^\top y -\sum_{j\ne i}x_i^\top x_jb_j, \]

which is the marker’s cross-product with the residual excluding its own current effect. BayesC-style inclusion and effect updates depend on \(s_i\), \(ww_i\), the residual variance, and prior parameters.

If marker \(i\)’s effect changes by

\[ \Delta b_i=b_i^{\mathrm{new}}-b_i^{\mathrm{old}}, \]

then

\[ r^{\mathrm{new}} = r^{\mathrm{old}}-XX_{\cdot i}\Delta b_i. \]

The CSR implementation performs the diagonal part directly:

\[ r_i^{\mathrm{new}} = r_i^{\mathrm{old}}-ww_i\Delta b_i, \]

then updates retained LD neighbors using sparse off-diagonal \(x_j^\top x_i\) values. This avoids multiplying by a dense \(XX\) matrix on every marker update.

The Identity \(XXb=wy-r\)

Rearranging the residual-score definition gives

\[ XXb=wy-r. \]

This identity is useful beyond marker updates. It permits likelihood, genetic-variance, and annotation-contribution calculations from summary statistics and the current marker effects.

Residual sum of squares

Starting from

\[ \operatorname{SSE} = yy-2b^\top wy+b^\top XXb \]

and substituting \(XXb=wy-r\) yields

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{SSE} &= yy-2b^\top wy+b^\top(wy-r)\\ &= yy-b^\top(wy+r). \end{aligned} \]

The single-trait CSR residual-variance update uses this form. It combines yy with the current b, wy, and residual score r without returning to individual-level observations.

Genetic variance

For centered genotypes, the realized genetic value is \(g=Xb\). Its quadratic form is

\[ g^\top g = b^\top XXb = b^\top(wy-r). \]

The corresponding sample-scaled genetic-variance quantity is

\[ \widehat V_G = \frac{1}{n}b^\top(wy-r). \]

This is the identity used by the CSR implementation to calculate genetic variance from the current effects and residual scores.

Genotype Scaling, Allele Frequencies, and ww

Summary statistics and LD are meaningful only relative to the genotype matrix that produced them. Let \(g_{ki}\in\{0,1,2\}\) be the dosage for individual \(k\) at marker \(i\), and let \(p_i\) be the supplied or estimated allele frequency.

With bed_xtx_xty(..., scale = TRUE), the implemented marker coding is

\[ x_{ki} = \frac{g_{ki}-2p_i} {\sqrt{2p_i(1-p_i)}}. \]

Missing BED genotypes are mapped to zero on this standardized scale, which is mean imputation under the supplied allele frequency. For nondegenerate markers and a compatible sample, the expected marker variance is approximately one, so

\[ ww_i=x_i^\top x_i \]

is often near the number of used individuals. It is not assumed to equal exactly \(n\): realized allele counts, missingness, external allele frequencies, and sample selection affect it.

With scale = FALSE, the implementation uses raw dosage coding and mean imputes missing values to \(2p_i\). This path is intentionally uncentered. Consequently, cross-products and regression interpretation differ from the standardized path unless centering is handled elsewhere.

Allele frequencies therefore define more than metadata. They determine the genotype matrix used in wy, ww, and LD. Supplying inconsistent allele frequencies to sufficient-statistic and sparse-LD construction changes the implied \(X\).

Dense LD, Correlation LD, and Sparse Cross-Products

The dense genotype cross-product matrix has entries

\[ XX_{ij}=x_i^\top x_j. \]

Its diagonal is supplied through ww. The disk-backed CSR files produced by the sparse-LD workflow store retained correlations \(r_{ij}\), not full cross-products. When the CSR sampler reads them, it reconstructs retained off-diagonal cross-products as

\[ \widetilde{XX}_{ij} = r_{ij}\sqrt{ww_iww_j}. \]

The sampler symmetrizes the retained off-diagonal entries for marker-wise updates. It handles the diagonal separately using ww; diagonal treatment is therefore part of the interface contract and should not be recreated by manually inserting assumed values into the disk files.

With all correlations retained and compatible ww, the reconstruction can represent the corresponding dense \(XX\). In practice, sparse-LD construction uses thresholds and windows, producing

\[ \widetilde{XX}\approx XX. \]

The residual score maintained by the sparse sampler is consequently

\[ \widetilde r = wy-\widetilde{XX}b. \]

Weak or distant omitted LD does not propagate effect changes through this approximate residual score. This approximation is the principal statistical difference between a complete cross-product representation and thresholded CSR LD.

See Technical sparse LD and CSR for storage, thresholding, and disk-contract details.

Computing Statistics With bed_xtx_xty()

bed_xtx_xty() computes sufficient statistics directly from selected markers in one PLINK BED file. A short call is:

stats <- bed_xtx_xty(
  bed_file = Glist$bedfiles[chr],
  n = Glist$n,
  cls = cls,
  af = Glist$af[[chr]][cls],
  y = y,
  rows = NULL,
  scale = TRUE
)

Its principal inputs have the following contracts:

  • bed_file identifies the PLINK BED file.
  • n is the number of individuals represented by that BED file.
  • cls contains positive one-based BED marker indices in the desired model order.
  • af contains allele frequencies in exactly the cls order. If omitted, the implementation computes frequencies from selected individuals.
  • y is an \(n_{\mathrm{used}}\times n_t\) phenotype matrix.
  • rows optionally selects unique one-based BED rows; when supplied, y must contain exactly those selected individuals in that order.
  • scale selects standardized or raw-dosage marker coding.

For each trait \(t\), the function returns:

\[ wy_t=X^\top y_t, \qquad ww_t=\operatorname{diag}(X^\top X), \qquad yy_t=y_t^\top y_t. \]

The same ww vector is repeated across traits because the current function uses one selected genotype matrix \(X\) for all phenotype columns. Additional returned fields include xx, allele frequencies used, row-selection information, sample and marker counts, scaling status, and summaries of marker diagonal cross-products.

The output order is the input cls order. The function does not infer the intended biological marker order from IDs, so that ordering must be established before the call.

Combining bed_xtx_xty() With Sparse LD

The sufficient-statistics and LD branches must describe the same genotype matrix:

same BED marker selection and order
+ same selected individuals
+ same allele frequencies and scaling
    -> compatible wy, ww, yy, and sparse LD

make_sparseLD() constructs retained marker correlations from BED data, writes them under a sparse-LD prefix, and records the metadata in Glist$sparseLD. It must use the same marker ordering, sample rows, allele frequencies, and scaling convention used to create the sufficient statistics. The lower-level writer behind this workflow is sparseLD_stream_CSR().

The central workflow handoff is:

BED + phenotype
  -> make_stats()     -> wy, ww, yy, n
  -> make_sparseLD()  -> Glist$sparseLD
  -> stblr_csr(stats = stats, Glist = Glist)

The complete operational sequence is documented in Sparse-LD and BED workflows.

How stblr_csr() Consumes the Statistics

stblr_csr() accepts:

  • stats$wy, one marker vector per trait;
  • stats$ww, one positive marker-diagonal vector per trait;
  • stats$yy, one phenotype sum of squares per trait;
  • \(n\), supplied explicitly or taken from stats$n;
  • Glist$sparseLD$prefix, or an explicit ld_prefix, identifying the compatible disk-backed sparse LD; and
  • prior, variance-update, scheduling, and MCMC controls.

The wrapper uses yy/(n-1) to initialize phenotype-variance-related prior scales. The native sampler validates marker and trait dimensions, reconstructs retained off-diagonal cross-products from LD correlations and ww, initializes or rebuilds

\[ r=wy-\widetilde{XX}b, \]

and performs marker and variance updates from the resulting summary-statistic state.

For the current shared-LD single-trait-per-trait CSR path, ww and \(n\) must agree across traits. This reflects one common genotype sample and scaling scheme. Supplying trait-specific summary statistics from materially different samples requires a model and LD contract that explicitly supports those differences.

Multi-Trait Summary-Statistic Notation

For \(n_t\) traits, collect phenotypes in

\[ Y= \begin{bmatrix} y_1 & \cdots & y_{n_t} \end{bmatrix} \]

and marker effects in an \(m\times n_t\) matrix

\[ B= \begin{bmatrix} b_1 & \cdots & b_{n_t} \end{bmatrix}. \]

The individual-level model is

\[ Y=XB+E_Y, \]

where the rows of \(E_Y\) may have residual covariance matrix \(\Sigma_e\). The central multi-trait cross-products are

\[ WY=X^\top Y \]

and

\[ SSY=Y^\top Y. \]

Column \(t\) of \(WY\) is \(wy_t=X^\top y_t\). Entry \(SSY_{ts}=y_t^\top y_s\) is the observed cross-trait phenotype product among individuals shared by traits \(t\) and \(s\).

For a common \(X\), define the marker-score matrix

\[ R=X^\top(Y-XB)=WY-XXB. \]

Trait by trait,

\[ XXb_t=wy_t-r_t. \]

This permits a symmetric genetic covariance estimate:

\[ \widehat G_{ts} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{n_tn_s}} \left[ b_t^\top(wy_s-r_s) + b_s^\top(wy_t-r_t) \right]. \]

When \(t=s\), this reduces to the single-trait genetic-variance expression. When all traits use the same \(n\), the denominator becomes \(n\).

Cross-Trait Phenotype Products and Sample Overlap

The diagonal of SSY contains the single-trait values:

\[ SSY_{tt}=yy_t. \]

The off-diagonals contain information not recoverable from separate yy values:

\[ SSY_{ts} = \sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{ts}}y_{it}y_{is}, \]

where \(\mathcal I_{ts}\) is the set of individuals observed for both traits. These terms are required for a full residual covariance update because

\[ (Y-XB)^\top(Y-XB) = SSY -Y^\top XB -B^\top X^\top Y +B^\top XXB. \]

With individual-level data, SSY and overlap counts are directly observable. With summary statistics, they must be supplied or reconstructed. If \(N_{ts}=|\mathcal I_{ts}|\) and the cross-trait phenotype covariance is \(\sigma_{ts}\), then under random missingness and covariance homogeneity,

\[ E(SSY_{ts})=N_{ts}\sigma_{ts}. \]

Given phenotype correlations \(R_y\) and approximate trait variances

\[ \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(y_t) \approx \frac{yy_t}{n_t}, \]

one can form

\[ \widehat{\sigma}_{ts} = R_{y,ts} \sqrt{ \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(y_t) \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(y_s) } \]

and reconstruct

\[ \widehat{SSY} = N\odot\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(Y), \]

forcing the diagonal to the observed yy values.

Full-overlap and fractional-overlap assumptions define different \(N\) matrices and therefore different reconstructed likelihood information. Missing or incorrect cross-trait information limits residual covariance inference even when every trait has valid marginal summary statistics. The complete derivation and sensitivity considerations belong in Technical multi-trait sample overlap.

Residual and Genetic Covariance From Summary Scores

For a joint multi-trait model, the residual cross-product matrix can be constructed from SSY, \(WY\), current effects, and residual scores. One orientation is

\[ \widehat S_e = SSY -B^\top WY -WY^\top B +B^\top XXB. \]

Using \(XXB=WY-R\),

\[ \widehat S_e = SSY -B^\top WY -WY^\top B +B^\top(WY-R). \]

Finite precision, asymmetric inputs, or trait-specific constructions can make cross-trait expressions differ slightly by orientation. The native multi-trait CSR implementation symmetrizes off-diagonal residual and genetic covariance quantities before covariance updates.

These calculations require a compatible definition of \(X\), trait sample sizes, and cross-trait phenotype products. A vector of per-trait yy values supports separate trait-wise residual variance updates; it does not by itself identify a full residual covariance matrix.

Set-Specific and Weighted Genetic Contributions

The identity \(XXb_t=wy_t-r_t\) also supports marker-set summaries. For a marker set \(S_k\), define

\[ \widehat V_{G,k,t} = \frac{1}{n_t} \sum_{i\in S_k} \widehat b_{it}(wy_{it}-r_{it}). \]

The corresponding marker-level contribution is

\[ c_{it} = \frac{\widehat b_{it}(wy_{it}-r_{it})}{n_t}. \]

An optional posterior-inclusion-weighted version is

\[ c^{\mathrm{weighted}}_{it} = \frac{ \widehat{dm}_{it}\widehat b_{it}(wy_{it}-r_{it}) }{n_t}. \]

These are useful descriptive summaries for annotations or genomic regions. For disjoint sets, their sums can approximate a partition of the fitted genetic-variance expression. For overlapping sets, a marker contributes to every set containing it, so set totals are attributions rather than an additive partition.

The formulas also mix posterior summaries nonlinearly when posterior mean bm, PIP dm, and a returned residual score are combined. They should not be confused with exact posterior expectations unless calculated draw by draw.

Exact Equivalence and Practical Differences

Suppose the following are all exact and compatible:

  • the same centered or otherwise identically coded \(X\);
  • the same phenotype \(y\);
  • complete \(wy=X^\top y\), \(yy=y^\top y\), and \(XX=X^\top X\);
  • the same prior, initialization, and variance model; and
  • an exact implementation of the same update schedule.

Then individual-level and summary-statistics marker updates can target the same model family because their likelihood calculations are algebraically equivalent.

Practical BED and CSR fits are not expected to be numerically identical:

  • sparse LD omits weak or distant off-diagonal entries;
  • stored correlations and reconstructed cross-products use finite precision;
  • BED and CSR implementations can maintain residuals differently;
  • update scheduling and marker order can differ;
  • prior and residual-adjustment settings may differ; and
  • MCMC output contains Monte Carlo variation.

Agreement should therefore be assessed through posterior patterns, predictions, variance summaries, and sensitivity analyses rather than equality of individual draws.

Validation and Diagnostics

Before fitting a summary-statistics model, check:

  1. Every trait’s wy vector has length \(m\).
  2. Every trait’s ww vector has length \(m\), contains finite positive values, and is compatible with the LD scaling.
  3. yy has one value per trait for parallel single-trait fitting, or SSY is a valid \(n_t\times n_t\) matrix for a full joint multi-trait likelihood.
  4. The supplied \(n\) is the sample size used to calculate the corresponding statistics; trait-specific \(n_t\) values and overlap assumptions are recorded where relevant.
  5. Sparse-LD dimensions equal \(m\times m\), and its marker order matches wy and ww.
  6. cls, allele frequencies, rows, and scale choices match between bed_xtx_xty() and sparse-LD construction.
  7. Marker names, annotation rows, and posterior output rows use the same order.
  8. Phenotypes are centered or the intercept/centering convention is otherwise explicit.
  9. ww magnitude is plausible for the coding and sample size.
  10. LD thresholds, windows, precision, and reference-sample provenance are recorded.

Useful diagnostics include inspecting stats$n, stats$m, stats$scale, stats$min_xx, stats$mean_xx, and stats$max_xx; reading CSR metadata with sparseLD_read_CSR(); and comparing BED-direct and CSR fits when the same individual-level data are available.

Limitations

Summary-statistics inference cannot recover information that was not retained or supplied.

  • LD compatibility: wy and ww from one sample or scaling convention combined with LD from another imply an approximate and potentially biased likelihood.
  • LD-reference mismatch: ancestry, allele frequencies, imputation quality, marker filtering, and sample selection can make reference LD differ from study LD.
  • Sparsification: thresholding and windowing deliberately remove off-diagonal information.
  • Phenotype preprocessing: unrecorded centering, scaling, covariate adjustment, or residualization changes the meaning of wy and yy.
  • Multi-trait information: marginal trait statistics do not identify cross-trait phenotype products or sample overlap.
  • Overlap assumptions: incorrect \(N\) or phenotype covariance assumptions can distort full residual covariance inference.
  • Posterior summaries: set contributions calculated from posterior means do not automatically propagate full posterior uncertainty.

These limitations should be treated as model-input assumptions, not merely implementation details.

CSR LD.

See [Technical sparse LD and CSR](technical_sparse_ld_csr.html) for storage,
thresholding, and disk-contract details.

## Computing Statistics With `bed_xtx_xty()`

`bed_xtx_xty()` computes sufficient statistics directly from selected markers
in one PLINK BED file. A short call is:

```r
stats <- bed_xtx_xty(
  bed_file = Glist$bedfiles[chr],
  n = Glist$n,
  cls = cls,
  af = Glist$af[[chr]][cls],
  y = y,
  rows = NULL,
  scale = TRUE
)
```

Its principal inputs have the following contracts:

- `bed_file` identifies the PLINK BED file.
- `n` is the number of individuals represented by that BED file.
- `cls` contains positive one-based BED marker indices in the desired model
  order.
- `af` contains allele frequencies in exactly the `cls` order. If omitted,
  the implementation computes frequencies from selected individuals.
- `y` is an $n_{\mathrm{used}}\times n_t$ phenotype matrix.
- `rows` optionally selects unique one-based BED rows; when supplied, `y` must
  contain exactly those selected individuals in that order.
- `scale` selects standardized or raw-dosage marker coding.

For each trait $t$, the function returns:

$$
wy_t=X^\top y_t,
\qquad
ww_t=\operatorname{diag}(X^\top X),
\qquad
yy_t=y_t^\top y_t.
$$

The same `ww` vector is repeated across traits because the current function
uses one selected genotype matrix $X$ for all phenotype columns. Additional
returned fields include `xx`, allele frequencies used, row-selection
information, sample and marker counts, scaling status, and summaries of
marker diagonal cross-products.

The output order is the input `cls` order. The function does not infer the
intended biological marker order from IDs, so that ordering must be
established before the call.

## Combining `bed_xtx_xty()` With Sparse LD

The sufficient-statistics and LD branches must describe the same genotype
matrix:

```text
same BED marker selection and order
+ same selected individuals
+ same allele frequencies and scaling
    -> compatible wy, ww, yy, and sparse LD
```

`make_sparseLD()` constructs retained marker correlations from BED data,
writes them under a sparse-LD prefix, and records the metadata in
`Glist$sparseLD`. It must use the same marker ordering, sample rows, allele
frequencies, and scaling convention used to create the sufficient statistics.
The lower-level writer behind this workflow is `sparseLD_stream_CSR()`.

The central workflow handoff is:

```text
BED + phenotype
  -> make_stats()     -> wy, ww, yy, n
  -> make_sparseLD()  -> Glist$sparseLD
  -> stblr_csr(stats = stats, Glist = Glist)
```

The complete operational sequence is documented in
[Sparse-LD and BED workflows](sparse_ld_and_bed_workflows.html).

## How `stblr_csr()` Consumes the Statistics

`stblr_csr()` accepts:

- `stats$wy`, one marker vector per trait;
- `stats$ww`, one positive marker-diagonal vector per trait;
- `stats$yy`, one phenotype sum of squares per trait;
- $n$, supplied explicitly or taken from `stats$n`;
- `Glist$sparseLD$prefix`, or an explicit `ld_prefix`, identifying the
  compatible disk-backed sparse LD; and
- prior, variance-update, scheduling, and MCMC controls.

The wrapper uses `yy/(n-1)` to initialize phenotype-variance-related prior
scales. The native sampler validates marker and trait dimensions, reconstructs
retained off-diagonal cross-products from LD correlations and `ww`, initializes
or rebuilds

$$
r=wy-\widetilde{XX}b,
$$

and performs marker and variance updates from the resulting summary-statistic
state.

For the current shared-LD single-trait-per-trait CSR path, `ww` and $n$ must
agree across traits. This reflects one common genotype sample and scaling
scheme. Supplying trait-specific summary statistics from materially different
samples requires a model and LD contract that explicitly supports those
differences.

## Multi-Trait Summary-Statistic Notation

For $n_t$ traits, collect phenotypes in

$$
Y=
\begin{bmatrix}
y_1 & \cdots & y_{n_t}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

and marker effects in an $m\times n_t$ matrix

$$
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
b_1 & \cdots & b_{n_t}
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The individual-level model is

$$
Y=XB+E_Y,
$$

where the rows of $E_Y$ may have residual covariance matrix
$\Sigma_e$. The central multi-trait cross-products are

$$
WY=X^\top Y
$$

and

$$
SSY=Y^\top Y.
$$

Column $t$ of $WY$ is $wy_t=X^\top y_t$. Entry
$SSY_{ts}=y_t^\top y_s$ is the observed cross-trait phenotype product among
individuals shared by traits $t$ and $s$.

For a common $X$, define the marker-score matrix

$$
R=X^\top(Y-XB)=WY-XXB.
$$

Trait by trait,

$$
XXb_t=wy_t-r_t.
$$

This permits a symmetric genetic covariance estimate:

$$
\widehat G_{ts}
=
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n_tn_s}}
\left[
b_t^\top(wy_s-r_s)
+
b_s^\top(wy_t-r_t)
\right].
$$

When $t=s$, this reduces to the single-trait genetic-variance expression.
When all traits use the same $n$, the denominator becomes $n$.

## Cross-Trait Phenotype Products and Sample Overlap

The diagonal of `SSY` contains the single-trait values:

$$
SSY_{tt}=yy_t.
$$

The off-diagonals contain information not recoverable from separate `yy`
values:

$$
SSY_{ts}
=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{ts}}y_{it}y_{is},
$$

where $\mathcal I_{ts}$ is the set of individuals observed for both traits.
These terms are required for a full residual covariance update because

$$
(Y-XB)^\top(Y-XB)
=
SSY
-Y^\top XB
-B^\top X^\top Y
+B^\top XXB.
$$

With individual-level data, `SSY` and overlap counts are directly observable.
With summary statistics, they must be supplied or reconstructed. If
$N_{ts}=|\mathcal I_{ts}|$ and the cross-trait phenotype covariance is
$\sigma_{ts}$, then under random missingness and covariance homogeneity,

$$
E(SSY_{ts})=N_{ts}\sigma_{ts}.
$$

Given phenotype correlations $R_y$ and approximate trait variances

$$
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(y_t)
\approx
\frac{yy_t}{n_t},
$$

one can form

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{ts}
=
R_{y,ts}
\sqrt{
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(y_t)
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(y_s)
}
$$

and reconstruct

$$
\widehat{SSY}
=
N\odot\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(Y),
$$

forcing the diagonal to the observed `yy` values.

Full-overlap and fractional-overlap assumptions define different $N$
matrices and therefore different reconstructed likelihood information.
Missing or incorrect cross-trait information limits residual covariance
inference even when every trait has valid marginal summary statistics. The
complete derivation and sensitivity considerations belong in
[Technical multi-trait sample overlap](technical_multitrait_overlap.html).

## Residual and Genetic Covariance From Summary Scores

For a joint multi-trait model, the residual cross-product matrix can be
constructed from `SSY`, $WY$, current effects, and residual scores. One
orientation is

$$
\widehat S_e
=
SSY
-B^\top WY
-WY^\top B
+B^\top XXB.
$$

Using $XXB=WY-R$,

$$
\widehat S_e
=
SSY
-B^\top WY
-WY^\top B
+B^\top(WY-R).
$$

Finite precision, asymmetric inputs, or trait-specific constructions can make
cross-trait expressions differ slightly by orientation. The native multi-trait
CSR implementation symmetrizes off-diagonal residual and genetic covariance
quantities before covariance updates.

These calculations require a compatible definition of $X$, trait sample
sizes, and cross-trait phenotype products. A vector of per-trait `yy` values
supports separate trait-wise residual variance updates; it does not by itself
identify a full residual covariance matrix.

## Set-Specific and Weighted Genetic Contributions

The identity $XXb_t=wy_t-r_t$ also supports marker-set summaries. For a
marker set $S_k$, define

$$
\widehat V_{G,k,t}
=
\frac{1}{n_t}
\sum_{i\in S_k}
\widehat b_{it}(wy_{it}-r_{it}).
$$

The corresponding marker-level contribution is

$$
c_{it}
=
\frac{\widehat b_{it}(wy_{it}-r_{it})}{n_t}.
$$

An optional posterior-inclusion-weighted version is

$$
c^{\mathrm{weighted}}_{it}
=
\frac{
\widehat{dm}_{it}\widehat b_{it}(wy_{it}-r_{it})
}{n_t}.
$$

These are useful descriptive summaries for annotations or genomic regions.
For disjoint sets, their sums can approximate a partition of the fitted
genetic-variance expression. For overlapping sets, a marker contributes to
every set containing it, so set totals are attributions rather than an
additive partition.

The formulas also mix posterior summaries nonlinearly when posterior mean
`bm`, PIP `dm`, and a returned residual score are combined. They should not be
confused with exact posterior expectations unless calculated draw by draw.

## Exact Equivalence and Practical Differences

Suppose the following are all exact and compatible:

- the same centered or otherwise identically coded $X$;
- the same phenotype $y$;
- complete $wy=X^\top y$, $yy=y^\top y$, and $XX=X^\top X$;
- the same prior, initialization, and variance model; and
- an exact implementation of the same update schedule.

Then individual-level and summary-statistics marker updates can target the
same model family because their likelihood calculations are algebraically
equivalent.

Practical BED and CSR fits are not expected to be numerically identical:

- sparse LD omits weak or distant off-diagonal entries;
- stored correlations and reconstructed cross-products use finite precision;
- BED and CSR implementations can maintain residuals differently;
- update scheduling and marker order can differ;
- prior and residual-adjustment settings may differ; and
- MCMC output contains Monte Carlo variation.

Agreement should therefore be assessed through posterior patterns,
predictions, variance summaries, and sensitivity analyses rather than equality
of individual draws.

## Validation and Diagnostics

Before fitting a summary-statistics model, check:

1. Every trait's `wy` vector has length $m$.
2. Every trait's `ww` vector has length $m$, contains finite positive values,
   and is compatible with the LD scaling.
3. `yy` has one value per trait for parallel single-trait fitting, or `SSY` is
   a valid $n_t\times n_t$ matrix for a full joint multi-trait likelihood.
4. The supplied $n$ is the sample size used to calculate the corresponding
   statistics; trait-specific $n_t$ values and overlap assumptions are
   recorded where relevant.
5. Sparse-LD dimensions equal $m\times m$, and its marker order matches
   `wy` and `ww`.
6. `cls`, allele frequencies, `rows`, and `scale` choices match between
   `bed_xtx_xty()` and sparse-LD construction.
7. Marker names, annotation rows, and posterior output rows use the same order.
8. Phenotypes are centered or the intercept/centering convention is otherwise
   explicit.
9. `ww` magnitude is plausible for the coding and sample size.
10. LD thresholds, windows, precision, and reference-sample provenance are
    recorded.

Useful diagnostics include inspecting `stats$n`, `stats$m`, `stats$scale`,
`stats$min_xx`, `stats$mean_xx`, and `stats$max_xx`; reading CSR metadata with
`sparseLD_read_CSR()`; and comparing BED-direct and CSR fits when the same
individual-level data are available.

## Limitations

Summary-statistics inference cannot recover information that was not retained
or supplied.

- **LD compatibility:** `wy` and `ww` from one sample or scaling convention
  combined with LD from another imply an approximate and potentially biased
  likelihood.
- **LD-reference mismatch:** ancestry, allele frequencies, imputation quality,
  marker filtering, and sample selection can make reference LD differ from
  study LD.
- **Sparsification:** thresholding and windowing deliberately remove
  off-diagonal information.
- **Phenotype preprocessing:** unrecorded centering, scaling, covariate
  adjustment, or residualization changes the meaning of `wy` and `yy`.
- **Multi-trait information:** marginal trait statistics do not identify
  cross-trait phenotype products or sample overlap.
- **Overlap assumptions:** incorrect $N$ or phenotype covariance assumptions
  can distort full residual covariance inference.
- **Posterior summaries:** set contributions calculated from posterior means
  do not automatically propagate full posterior uncertainty.

These limitations should be treated as model-input assumptions, not merely
implementation details.
